## 1. Overview

Assignment 1 requires an academic literature review on a selected computing or engineering research topic. The paper must explain and synthesize the current state of knowledge, critically evaluate relevant research, identify gaps or unresolved issues, and propose directions for future research.

This is not a collection of article summaries. The literature should be organized around themes, methods, findings, agreements, disagreements, limitations, and research gaps.

## 2. Assessment Context

- **Assignment weight:** 40% of the module
- **Expected length:** Approximately 4,000 words, excluding references
- **Indicative length:** About 6-12 pages of text
- **Submission format:** Microsoft Word document
- **Formatting:** Times New Roman, 12-point font, single line spacing
- **Referencing:** APA style
- **Similarity requirement:** Turnitin score below 10%
- **Submission and deadline:** Submit through Moodle; consult Moodle for the applicable date

## 3. Required Evidence Base

- Use at least **15 academic articles**.
- Articles should generally be no older than five years and should include publications from the current year where available.
- Prioritize peer-reviewed journal articles and conference papers. Suitable academic books may also be used.
- Popular magazines and newspaper articles do not count as journal articles.
- Ordinary websites are generally unsuitable unless they host a peer-reviewed publication or belong to an appropriate authoritative body.
- Avoid direct quotations. Paraphrase accurately and cite the original source.

## 4. Literature Review Paper Table of Contents

### 4.1. Cover Page

Include:

- Paper title
- Student name and TP number
- Intake code and date
- Module name and code
- Module lecturer's name
- Supervisor's name

### 4.2. Abstract and Keywords

Summarize the topic, purpose, coverage, major findings, and main conclusion.

### 4.3. Introduction

- Introduce the topic for a general scientific audience.
- Explain why the topic is important.
- State the paper's main purpose or objective.
- Define any necessary specialist terms.

### 4.4. Thematic Sections

- Organize the review into approximately three to five meaningful themes where appropriate.
- Compare and contrast findings across sources.
- Discuss research methods, strengths, limitations, consistencies, and contradictions.
- Use headings and subheadings to make the synthesis easy to follow.
- Do **not** create a section titled `Literature Review`, because the entire paper is a literature review.

### 4.5. Conclusion and Future Research

- Synthesize the overall findings in the student's own words.
- Explain what is currently known and what remains uncertain.
- Identify clear research gaps.
- Recommend specific and justified future studies.

### 4.6. References

- Include only works cited in the paper.
- Format all entries using APA style.

### 4.7. Literature Review Matrix

Include the completed literature review matrix with the submission.

## 5. Literature Review Matrix Fields

For each source, record at least:

| Field | What to capture |
|---|---|
| Full reference | Complete APA reference and publication details |
| Research question or objective | What the study attempted to determine |
| Methodology | Research design, data, sample, tools, and analysis |
| Main results | Important findings or issues reported by the authors |
| Conclusions | The authors' interpretation of the results |
| Relevance | How the paper contributes to this review's topic or argument |
| Critical appraisal | Strengths, weaknesses, limitations, and reliability |
| Theme | The thematic section in which the source may be synthesized |
| Future implications | Implications for research or practice |

## 6. Marking Scheme

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Paper structure, English, grammar, and spelling | 15% |
| Scope and depth of research | 20% |
| Literature review technical content | 25% |
| Critical analysis, discussion, and evaluation | 25% |
| Citations and references | 15% |

The strongest emphasis is on technical literature coverage and critical analysis. Together, these account for 50% of the marks.

## 7. What a High-Quality Submission Should Demonstrate

- A focused and academically suitable topic
- Systematic searching across multiple scholarly databases
- Thoughtful combinations of search terms
- Broad and sufficiently deep coverage of recent literature
- Thematic synthesis rather than author-by-author reporting
- Critical appraisal of most or all cited studies
- Clear comparison of agreements, disagreements, methods, and limitations
- A well-supported research gap and useful future-research direction
- Accurate, recent, reliable, and professionally formatted citations
- Professional structure, consistent language, and careful proofreading

## 8. Recommended Working Process

1. Confirm the research topic and its boundaries with the supervisor.
2. Define search terms, synonyms, inclusion criteria, and exclusion criteria.
3. Search suitable scholarly databases and record the search process.
4. Select at least 15 strong, recent, and directly relevant sources.
5. Complete the literature review matrix before drafting.
6. Group the evidence into themes and prepare a paper outline.
7. Draft each thematic section by comparing and synthesizing multiple sources.
8. Develop the conclusion, research gap, and future-research recommendations.
9. Verify every citation and APA reference.
10. Revise several times for reasoning, structure, clarity, grammar, and similarity.
